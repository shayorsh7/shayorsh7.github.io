01 / ABOUT ME

Curiosity at the intersection of light and electronics.

I am an Electrical and Electronic Engineering educator and emerging researcher interested in how photonic devices can be engineered for faster, more flexible optical systems.

My recent collaborative work investigates thin-film lithium-niobate (TFLN) racetrack resonators, electro-optic tuning, engineered photon lifetime, optical filtering, and reconfigurable 2 × 2 switching. These projects use numerical optical and electrostatic models to understand trade-offs among optical loss, coupling, extinction, and tunability.

I earned my B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the Islamic University of Technology (IUT) with a CGPA of 3.97/4.00 in 2025. I currently teach in the Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Daffodil International University.

Electro-optic modulationUnder review · Optics Express

Cladding-assisted inline-transition and photon-lifetime engineering in a racetrack modulator

Investigating how engineered intracavity attenuation and asymmetric bus coupling can broaden optical resonances while preserving strong spectral contrast in X-cut TFLN.

63.4 GHzSimulated optical linewidth20.46 pm/VResonance tuning39.1 dBStatic modulation ER
Research details +

The numerical design combines SiO₂-clad active arms with polymer-clad passive bends and four abrupt inline transitions. Bayesian geometry optimization and independently selected input/drop bus gaps balance photon lifetime, extinction, and insertion loss. These are simulated optical and static modulation results, not experimentally measured RF bandwidth.

FIGURE FROM FILTER MANUSCRIPT
Optical filteringUnder review · Optics & Laser Technology

Near-orthogonal electrical control of center wavelength and notch spacing

A cascaded, bus-connected dual-racetrack filter using common-mode and differential bias to tune two spectral properties nearly independently.

53.66 dBSimulated notch rejection4.561 GHz/VSpacing tuning
Research details +

The numerical study reports a 45.607 GHz nominal notch spacing, 1.458 dB outer-passband insertion loss, 18.34 pm/V center-wavelength tuning, and resolved dual notches under ±20 nm joint coupling-gap variations. The resonators are bus-mediated; no direct racetrack-to-racetrack evanescent coupling is assumed.

05 / ENGINEERING PRACTICE

Technical projects

Selected undergraduate hardware projects from my EEE coursework at IUT. The photographs and downloadable project reports are extracted from my undergraduate project portfolio.

01 / DIGITAL ELECTRONICS · 2023

SAP-1 Computer Architecture

Studied and implemented the modular architecture of an 8-bit Simple-As-Possible (SAP-1) computer, including the data bus, program counter, memory-address register, 16 × 8-bit memory, arithmetic logic unit, accumulator, instruction register and controller/sequencer. The portfolio documents circuit simulation, an instruction-level fetch/execute sequence, and physical PCB implementations of individual modules.

Technical highlights: 8-bit shared bus with tri-state outputs, 4-bit program counter, five-instruction set (LDA, ADD, SUB, OUT, HLT), and manual/automatic clock operation.

02 / EMBEDDED SYSTEMS · 2023

Project Nema — Automated Aquarium

Developed an Arduino Uno-based aquarium monitoring and control prototype integrating pH, water-temperature and ultrasonic water-level sensing. The system displays measurements on an OLED, uses GSM messages for remote alerts and commands, and coordinates a heater, water pumps and a servo-driven automatic feeder.

Technical highlights: Sensor-actuator integration, SMS-based monitoring, threshold-controlled heating and pumping, and scheduled feeding. The report also records challenges involving sensor calibration, response latency and GSM communication.

View aquarium project report (PDF) ↗
03 / MICROCONTROLLER SYSTEM DESIGN · 2023

Digital Alarm Clock

Designed and implemented a digital alarm clock using an AT89C52 microcontroller, LCD, 4 × 4 keypad, buzzer and a motor-driven water-spray mechanism. The software uses Timer 1 interrupts for timekeeping and keypad-based modes for clock setting, alarm setting and interactive dismissal.

Technical highlights: HH:MM:SS display, 1-, 2- and 3-minute snooze options, a secret-key alarm dismissal mechanism and keypad debouncing. Multi-alarm support was explored but remained a limitation of the prototype.

View alarm-clock project report (PDF) ↗
04 / POWER ELECTRONICS · 2023

High-Voltage Power-Electronics Demonstration

Explored voltage conversion and pulse generation in a coursework prototype, gaining practical experience with power-electronics concepts, circuit analysis and the importance of electrical safety. This project is described at a high level without publishing construction details of a high-voltage stun device.

Focus: Power conversion principles, prototype observation and high-voltage safety considerations.
